Scope

What the plan covers

People and beds

Workforce curves by trade and quarter, shuttle networks and node strategy, parking as the regulator, accommodation demand against the market's real bed stock — with decide-by dates for every remedy that has a lead time.

Materials and modes

Port, rail and road doctrine set by evidence, not habit. Abnormal-load corridors, consolidation and vehicle booking, delivery windows that respect the network you actually have — including the one being rebuilt around you.

Inside the fence

Gate strategy, laydown with expiry dates, welfare sized to the overlap case not the average, commissioning treated as its own logistics phase — and one logistics authority across every building and package.

The concurrency

Every neighbouring programme that draws on your labour, beds and roads, summed into one combined curve. The collision map most consents never require — and most programmes never see until it costs them.

Why it's different

Delivery-side, not consent-side

Built by an operator

Not adapted from a transport assessment. The plan is written by the person who runs parking, bussing, welfare and access daily at megaproject scale — and it reads like it.

Built from the record

Consents, programmes, published constraints and your own data, every figure labelled sourced, derived or illustrative. Challengeable line by line — that is the point.

Built to stay true

Every plan can roll into the Headroom standing monthly pack: actuals versus forecast, reconciled in plain sight, breach dates and costed remedies kept current as the programme moves.
